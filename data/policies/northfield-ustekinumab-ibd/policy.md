### 1. Purpose

This policy describes when Northfield Health Plan considers ustekinumab products medically necessary for members 18 years of age and older with Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis. Requests for members under 18 are reviewed under policy NHP-CCP-RX-0413 (Ustekinumab Products, Pediatric) and are outside the scope of this policy. Coverage is subject to the terms of the member's benefit plan. Where this policy and the member's plan documents differ, the plan documents control.

### 2. Benefit determination

Ustekinumab therapy for inflammatory bowel disease is given in two phases, and Northfield processes them under different benefits:

- **Intravenous induction dose.** The single weight-based intravenous dose is administered in a clinic or infusion center and is covered under the **medical benefit**. Requests are submitted by the administering provider through the Northfield provider portal.
- **Subcutaneous maintenance doses.** Self-administered subcutaneous doses are covered under the **pharmacy benefit** and must be dispensed by a Northfield network specialty pharmacy. Requests are submitted through electronic prior authorization.

A single approval letter will reference both phases when both are requested together. An approval under one benefit does not create coverage under the other.

### 3. Criteria for initial authorization

Northfield considers ustekinumab medically necessary for initial therapy when **all** of the following are met and documented in the medical record:

**3.1 Diagnosis.** The member has a diagnosis of moderately to severely active Crohn's disease or moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis. Disease severity must be described by the treating provider (for example, by symptom burden, a validated disease activity index, or endoscopic severity).

**3.2 Objective evidence of active disease.** The record includes at least one of the following, dated within the 12 months before the request:

- (a) an endoscopy, colonoscopy, or flexible sigmoidoscopy report describing active inflammation (such as ulceration, erosions, or friability); or
- (b) a fecal calprotectin result above the upper limit of the performing laboratory's reference range.

An elevated C-reactive protein or erythrocyte sedimentation rate alone does not satisfy this criterion. Imaging alone does not satisfy this criterion.

**3.3 Prescriber.** The medication is prescribed by, or in consultation with, a gastroenterologist.

**3.4 Age.** The member is 18 years of age or older.

**3.5 Prior therapy.** Both of the following are documented:

- (a) **Conventional therapy.** An inadequate response to, intolerance of, or contraindication to at least one conventional therapy. For this policy, conventional therapies are systemic corticosteroids, azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine, and methotrexate; for ulcerative colitis, oral aminosalicylates (such as mesalamine) also count. An inadequate response to corticosteroids includes steroid dependence (relapse during taper or within 3 months of stopping) and steroid-refractory disease. Budesonide counts as a corticosteroid for this purpose.
- (b) **Anti-TNF therapy.** An inadequate response to, intolerance of, or contraindication to at least one tumor necrosis factor (TNF) blocker: adalimumab or infliximab, including any biosimilar of either. Primary non-response, secondary loss of response, and intolerance each qualify. A contraindication must be specifically documented (for example, demyelinating disease or moderate to severe heart failure).

The record must name the drug, give approximate start and stop dates, and state why it was stopped. Prior therapy must be supported by pharmacy or medical claims, prescription records, or treating-provider chart notes. A treating-provider history without supporting records may be accepted on prescriber attestation. If claims or other records on file are inconsistent with the reported history, the request is referred for clinical review and is not approved on the reported history alone.

**3.6 Tuberculosis screening.** The member has a negative tuberculosis screening test (interferon-gamma release assay or tuberculin skin test) dated within the **12 months** before the request. An indeterminate interferon-gamma release assay is not a negative result. If the screening test is positive, the record must show that active tuberculosis has been excluded by chest imaging and that treatment for latent tuberculosis has been started.

**3.7 Hepatitis B screening.** Results of **both** hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) **and** total hepatitis B core antibody (anti-HBc) are on file. There is no lookback limit for this criterion. If either result is positive, the record must include a plan for monitoring or antiviral prophylaxis. Hepatitis B vaccination history, or a hepatitis B surface antibody (anti-HBs) result alone, does not satisfy this criterion. A test that has been ordered but not resulted does not satisfy this criterion.

**3.8 No combination advanced therapy.** Ustekinumab will not be used together with another biologic agent, a Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor (such as tofacitinib or upadacitinib), or a sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator (such as ozanimod or etrasimod). If the member is switching from one of these agents, the record must show that the prior agent has been or will be stopped.

**3.9 Dosing.** The requested dose follows the FDA-approved adult labeling for the indication:

- Induction: a single intravenous dose based on body weight: 260 mg for 55 kg or less; 390 mg for more than 55 kg up to 85 kg; 520 mg for more than 85 kg.
- Maintenance: 90 mg subcutaneously every 8 weeks, starting 8 weeks after the intravenous dose.

A current body weight (within 30 days of the request) must be documented for the induction dose. Maintenance intervals shorter than every 8 weeks are not approved on initial authorization.

**3.10 Preferred products.** Northfield's preferred ustekinumab products are **ustekinumab-aekn** and **ustekinumab-ttwe**, in both intravenous and subcutaneous forms. All other ustekinumab products, including the reference (originator) product, are non-preferred. A non-preferred product is covered only when the record documents one of the following:

- (a) an inadequate response to, or intolerance of, **both** preferred products; or
- (b) a contraindication or documented hypersensitivity to an inactive ingredient present in both preferred products and absent from the requested product.

Intolerance of one preferred product does not satisfy (a). The request must name the specific product; a request for "ustekinumab" without a product is incomplete.

### 4. Approval duration

Initial authorization: **6 months**, covering the induction dose and maintenance doses through the end of the approval period.

### 5. Continuation of therapy

Continuation requests require documentation of clinical response to therapy, such as improvement in symptoms, disease activity scores, biomarkers, or endoscopic findings compared with baseline. Requests for dose interval shortening to every 4 or 6 weeks require documentation of partial response or loss of response on the every-8-week interval. Continuation approvals are issued for 12 months.

### 6. Requests that are not medically necessary

Northfield does not consider ustekinumab medically necessary:

- in combination with another biologic, JAK inhibitor, or S1P receptor modulator for inflammatory bowel disease;
- at doses or frequencies above those in section 3.9 for initial therapy;
- when a non-preferred product is requested and the requirements of section 3.10 are not met.

### 7. Expedited review

A prescriber may request expedited review when waiting for a standard decision could seriously jeopardize the member's life, health, or ability to regain maximum function. The request must include the prescriber's statement of the clinical reason. Expedited pharmacy-benefit requests are decided within 24 hours of receipt of a complete request; standard pharmacy-benefit requests within 72 hours. Medical-benefit drug requests follow the timelines in the member's plan documents.

### 8. Documentation checklist for submitters

To avoid delays, submit:

- the most recent gastroenterology note describing disease severity;
- the endoscopy report or fecal calprotectin result used for section 3.2;
- a medication history covering prior conventional and anti-TNF therapy with dates and reasons for stopping;
- tuberculosis and hepatitis B results;
- current weight for induction dosing.
- the specific product requested and, for a non-preferred product, the documentation required by section 3.10.

### 9. Definitions

- **Inadequate response:** failure to reach or keep a meaningful clinical improvement after an adequate trial at a standard dose, as judged and documented by the treating provider.
- **Intolerance:** an adverse effect that required the drug to be stopped or reduced below an effective dose.
- **Secondary loss of response:** initial response followed by return of active disease while on therapy.
