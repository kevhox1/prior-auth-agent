SECTION A. OVERVIEW

Ustekinumab is an interleukin-12/23 antagonist. Calder Mutual covers ustekinumab reference product and its biosimilars on equal terms for adult members with moderately to severely active Crohn's disease (CD) or ulcerative colitis (UC) when the conditions in Section B are documented. This policy does not establish a preferred ustekinumab product; any FDA-approved ustekinumab product may be requested. Members under 18 are reviewed case by case by a Calder Mutual pharmacist and are not covered by this policy.

Benefit. The one-time intravenous induction infusion is billed by the administering facility or office and is reviewed under the member's MEDICAL benefit. Subcutaneous maintenance injections are dispensed by a specialty pharmacy and are reviewed under the PHARMACY benefit. Prior approval is required for each.

SECTION B. MEDICAL NECESSITY CRITERIA FOR INITIAL APPROVAL

Initial approval requires every one of items B.1 through B.9.

B.1 The member has moderately to severely active CD or UC, as stated by the prescriber in the clinical record.

B.2 Disease activity has been confirmed by direct visualization: an ileocolonoscopy, colonoscopy, or flexible sigmoidoscopy performed within the past 12 months that documents active mucosal inflammation. Laboratory markers, including fecal calprotectin, C-reactive protein, and sedimentation rate, do not meet this requirement on their own, and neither do cross-sectional imaging studies.

B.3 The prescriber is a gastroenterologist, or the prescriber has consulted with one about this treatment.

B.4 The member is at least 18 years old on the date of the request.

B.5 The member has tried at least one prior therapy for CD or UC, shown by either (a) or (b). One is enough:
  (a) a conventional agent (an oral or intravenous corticosteroid, budesonide, azathioprine, mercaptopurine, or methotrexate; for UC, an oral 5-aminosalicylate) that was stopped or found insufficient because of inadequate response, intolerance, or contraindication; or
  (b) a biologic or targeted synthetic agent for CD or UC (for example adalimumab, infliximab, vedolizumab, certolizumab, risankizumab, tofacitinib, upadacitinib, ozanimod) that was stopped or found insufficient because of inadequate response or intolerance, or a documented contraindication to TNF blockers.
  The record should identify the drug, roughly when it was used, and why it was stopped. Calder Mutual does not require a trial of a TNF blocker before ustekinumab.

B.6 Tuberculosis has been screened for with an interferon-gamma release assay or tuberculin skin test with a negative result in the past 6 months. An indeterminate or borderline result is not negative. A member with a positive screen qualifies only if the record shows a chest radiograph without active disease and that treatment for latent infection has begun.

B.7 Hepatitis B status is documented with results for hepatitis B surface antigen and total hepatitis B core antibody. These results may be of any age. A reactive result requires a documented plan for antiviral prophylaxis or monitoring. Surface antibody results and vaccination records are not sufficient. Pending orders are not sufficient.

B.8 Ustekinumab will not be combined with any other biologic, JAK inhibitor, or S1P receptor modulator for CD or UC. When the member is switching from one of these, the record shows the earlier agent is stopping.

B.9 The requested regimen is the labeled adult regimen: one weight-based intravenous induction infusion (260 mg up to and including 55 kg; 390 mg above 55 kg up to and including 85 kg; 520 mg above 85 kg), then 90 mg subcutaneously every 8 weeks beginning 8 weeks after the infusion. A body weight recorded in the 90 days before the request must be on file.

SECTION C. APPROVAL PERIOD

Initial approvals are issued for 12 months.

SECTION D. RENEWAL

Renewal requires prescriber documentation of a positive clinical response. Renewals are issued for 12 months. Requests for intervals shorter than every 8 weeks are reviewed by a Calder Mutual gastroenterologist.

SECTION E. URGENT REQUESTS

A prescriber who attests that the standard review time could seriously jeopardize the member's health may ask for urgent review. Urgent requests are decided within 24 hours of receiving all required information; standard requests within 72 hours (pharmacy benefit) or 7 calendar days (medical benefit).
